# Weekly News Digest — 2026 Week 28
> **Last Updated**: July 11, 2026

A roundup of Kubernetes, Amazon EKS, and CNCF ecosystem news published between July 4 and July 11, 2026, selected for relevance to this guide.

## This Week's News

### 1. etcd v3.7.0 Released

SIG etcd released etcd v3.7.0, shipping the long-requested RangeStream feature, removing the last remnants of the legacy v2store, and completing a major protobuf overhaul alongside several performance improvements.

- Source: [Announcing etcd v3.7.0](https://kubernetes.io/blog/2026/07/08/announcing-etcd-3.7/) (Kubernetes Blog, 2026-07-08)
- Related page: [Cluster Architecture](../core/01-cluster-architecture.md)

### 2. Navigating the ingress-nginx Retirement

The CNCF blog published a migration guide for life after the March 2026 retirement of the ingress-nginx controller. Staying on the retired controller carries serious operational risk — unpatched CVEs and no further feature updates — so teams should plan a move to Gateway API-based alternatives.

- Source: [Navigating the ingress-NGINX retirement](https://www.cncf.io/blog/2026/07/09/navigating-the-ingress-nginx-retirement/) (CNCF Blog, 2026-07-09)
- Related pages: [Gateway API](../networking/04-gateway-api.md), [AWS Load Balancer Controller](../networking/03-aws-lb-controller.md)

### 3. EKS Auto Mode Cuts GPU Management Fees by up to 60%

Effective July 1, 2026, EKS Auto Mode management fees are reduced by 35% for G-series instances and by 60% for P-series and AWS Trainium. The reductions apply automatically to all EKS Auto Mode clusters with no customer action required.

- Source: [Amazon EKS Auto Mode reduces GPU management fees by up to 60%](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/07/amazon-eks-auto-mode-gpu-price) (AWS What's New, 2026-07-07)
- Related page: [EKS Auto Mode Cost Management](../eks-auto-mode/06-cost-management.md)

### 4. Karpenter v1.14.0 Released

Karpenter's AWS provider v1.14.0 is out, adding CapacityBuffers API support, support for preview instance types, and the ability to set EnclaveOptions in launch templates, among other features and fixes.

- Source: [karpenter-provider-aws v1.14.0](https://github.com/aws/karpenter-provider-aws/releases/tag/v1.14.0) (GitHub Releases, 2026-07-10)
- Related page: [Karpenter](../autoscaling/02-karpenter.md)

### 5. Argo CD v3.4.5 Released

Argo CD published patch release v3.4.5 on the stable 3.4 track. The next minor release, v3.5.0, is also in release-candidate testing (rc2), so production clusters should keep following stable-channel patches.

- Source: [argo-cd v3.4.5](https://github.com/argoproj/argo-cd/releases/tag/v3.4.5) (GitHub Releases, 2026-07-09)
- Related page: [ArgoCD](../gitops/argocd/README.md)

### 6. CloudWatch Application Signals Auto-Captures Errors, Anomalies, and Deployments

CloudWatch Application Signals added Service Events, which automatically captures exception snapshots, latency anomalies, and deployment events. It works out of the box for applications instrumented with the ADOT SDKs or the CloudWatch Observability EKS add-on.

- Source: [CloudWatch Application Signals now automatically captures errors, performance anomalies, and deployment events](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/06/cloudwatch-service-events/) (AWS What's New, 2026-07-06)
- Related pages: [CloudWatch Metrics](../observability/metrics/04-cloudwatch-metrics.md), [EKS Monitoring and Logging](../eks/06-eks-monitoring-logging.md)

### 7. EMR on EKS Adds an Apache Spark Troubleshooting Agent

Amazon EMR on EKS now supports an AI-powered troubleshooting agent for Apache Spark workloads, helping diagnose job failures and performance issues on EKS.

- Source: [Amazon EMR on EKS now supports Apache Spark troubleshooting agent](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2026/07/amazon-emr-eks-spark-troubleshooting/) (AWS What's New, 2026-07-10)
- Related page: [Data on EKS Overview](../data-on-eks/README.md)

### 8. Evolving Platform Engineering for AI-Native Workloads

A CNCF blog post examines how platform engineering must evolve to support AI-native workloads — from GPU resource abstraction to self-service model serving — and what that means for internal developer platforms (IDPs).

- Source: [Evolving platform engineering for AI-native workloads](https://www.cncf.io/blog/2026/07/06/evolving-platform-engineering-for-ai-native-workloads/) (CNCF Blog, 2026-07-06)
- Related page: [Platform Engineering Overview](../platform-engineering/00-platform-engineering-overview.md)

### 9. Why Sandboxing Your AI Agent Is Not Enough

The CNCF blog argues that isolating AI agent workloads in a sandbox is insufficient on its own, and that defense in depth — network policies, least-privilege access, and more — is required. A useful security perspective for anyone running agent platforms on Kubernetes.

- Source: [Why sandboxing your agent is not enough](https://www.cncf.io/blog/2026/07/07/why-sandboxing-your-agent-is-not-enough/) (CNCF Blog, 2026-07-07)
- Related pages: [Agentic AI Platform on EKS](../ai-ml/03-agentic-ai-platform.md), [Runtime Security](../security/08-runtime-security.md)
